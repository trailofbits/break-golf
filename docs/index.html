How it works

A challenge fixes two worlds and the statement you must prove. You write an adversary and a proof that it tells them apart. The win is the proof — nothing is run or sampled. A submission is one declaration inhabiting a type the challenge pins in its own Challenge.lean, which the verifier builds its own copy of.

You supply
strategygiven the answers so far, the next query
verdictaccept or reject, read off the transcript
winsthe proof, at the pinned type
budget, advNum, advDenyour score, as Lean definitions the type is indexed by

Scoring

score = log₂( budget / advantage^e )

The security level, in bits, that your attack refutes. Lower is better; nothing is fixed in advance. Par is the designer's own claim, from the specification — under par is a break of it.

You declare budget, advNum and advDen in your own Lean, and the challenge type is indexed by them. Claim fewer queries or more advantage and the theorem becomes a stronger one your proof stops discharging; claim less than you can prove and the score gets worse. CI reads the numbers back out of Lean, so there is nothing to state and no direction to game in.

Submit

Opens a prefilled issue titled verify: <challenge>. Your score is not a form field — budget, advNum and advDen are definitions in your Lean, and the challenge type is indexed by them.
